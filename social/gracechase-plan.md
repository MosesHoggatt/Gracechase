## Audience system

Cadence this project can actually run: during a release month, the calendar above. Outside of one, two posts a week only when a real asset exists (a finished video, a lyric video, a new single, a Sunday song). A week with nothing gets nothing. The failure mode to avoid is a daily caption with no song behind it.

Content types, in the order they earn attention:

1. Story music video, Adam’s cut, one song. This is the format that produced 91k and 54k.
2. A 20–45 second piece of that video, for Reels, Facebook, and X, always pointing at the YouTube upload.
3. Lyric video for a song that will not get the full treatment. One or two per album, not eleven.
4. Credit post: writer, song, one lyric image, link. This is the in-between post.
5. Character post, after the cast list is in. The character is in the situation of one song. The caption still says the characters are fictional and the family wrote the lyric.
6. Sunday morning worship song from the non-Christmas catalog (Your Majesty, Set Me Free, Up to You) once Christmas is over. One song, not a service.

## Getting paid

DistroKid pays the recording side. Stores report on their own schedule; DistroKid’s help center says you typically see earnings from most services about three months after the streams, that they pass through 100% of what the stores send for ordinary streams and sales, and that you withdraw from the Bank (their public minimum is $6) through Tipalti. Optional Social Media Pack / YouTube Content ID is the exception: DistroKid keeps 20% of that UGC money and sends 80%. Switching distributors will not raise the per-stream rate. DistroKid’s own help page is also the one that clearly accepts AI music, with conditions: you own the rights, you do not impersonate someone, you do not upload mass-generated spam, and you do not infringe. Stores can still reject a release DistroKid accepted.

Do these in this order.

1. **Open the Bank this week.** Confirm a payout method exists, see which stores have ever reported, and see whether YouTube money is in there. DistroKid has said in public help text that YouTube earnings for July 2026 were late, and later notices mentioned July and August. If you opted into YouTube money, check that those months arrived. This is a lookup, and it tells us whether anything is paying.
2. **Register the songs as compositions.** DistroKid does not replace a PRO or The MLC. Pick one PRO (ASCAP or BMI, not both for the same writer). Register the human writers under the names you already print (Rebekah Shiloh Hoggatt, Ruth Hoggatt, Naomi Hoggatt, Adam Hoggatt, whoever actually wrote each song). Register the compositions, and register the same works with The MLC so US streaming mechanicals can match. Start with Let My Heart Find Christmas, To Hear the Angels Sing, I Still Care About Your Christmas, and This Is How You Christmas. This is the largest likely leak, and it is an afternoon plus a few minutes per new song.
3. **Check YouTube Partner Program in Studio.** The public bar is 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in the last 12 months (Shorts have a separate bar). The channel is over 1,000 subscribers. Watch hours are only visible in Studio. If you are eligible, apply. The 91k and 54k views may have earned nothing. On each realistic AI video, set YouTube’s altered-or-synthetic disclosure. YouTube’s help page says that disclosure does not by itself limit reach or monetization. A channel of interchangeable template videos can still fail the inauthentic-content rule. These story videos are specific enough to apply with; a lyric-video mill is not.
4. **Fix AI credits on the new releases.** In DistroKid, for a family lyric produced in Suno: the audio is AI-generated, the lyrics are not, and the artist is human. Gracechase is the family, not an AI persona. If “all of the audio” is checked, DistroKid also asks whether the artist name is a human or an AI persona. Answer human. Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube can show these credits. Marking the act as an AI persona is a different bucket.
5. **Look at the Spotify profile for an AI Persona badge.** Spotify’s 2026 badge is about a photorealistic identity that could be mistaken for a real person. It is not a label for “uses Suno.” Music from a badged AI Persona is kept out of recommendations unless the listener already follows. Your collage reads as a professional photo of 7 people and was made in Firefly and Gemini. Check Spotify for Artists. If a “Likely AI Persona” badge appears, appeal it and keep the real-people bio. Do not self-apply the badge. Prefer an avatar that reads as the project (a mark, or a clearly drawn character) on the DSP profile, and keep the realistic character stills for videos where the tools are listed.
6. **Content ID on every Gracechase release.** Social Media Pack is an annual charge per release, and DistroKid keeps 20%. November 4, 2026: Moses added it to Christmas Window and to the All I Need live single. Every new Gracechase release gets it. After Interstreet claims one of our videos, allowlist that video so the channel keeps the ad money. Do not dispute the claim.
7. **Leave the distributor alone.** A move will not increase the rate stores pay. If someone suggests another distributor, read that company’s current AI rules before you move a single release. Do not assume a Suno catalog is welcome everywhere DistroKid has allowed it.
8. **Future uploads: label name Gracechase Music**, songwriter names identical to the PRO registration, and AI credits filled in before you submit. The 2025 “7373006 Records DK” line can stay; re-delivering old albums to rename a label is not worth the churn.
9. **Confirm the traditional tracks.** Hark, Angels We Have Heard on High, and Jingle Bells need a straight answer in the DistroKid release: original composition, or a cover license. A new arrangement of a public-domain carol can be yours. A copyrighted arrangement cannot. Check it once so a claim does not land in December.
10. **Suno license, once.** Commercial distribution depends on Suno’s terms for the plan that actually generated the file. Confirm Christmas Window and the single were made on a plan that allows commercial release, and keep the download. Do this before the next album, not as a research project.
